### R11-01 — HIGH — §11.1 stop-condition judgement: the "patch identity **and** counts" combination is nominal

**Section:** §11.1 "Stop-condition check", §1.7.1 ("Neither line counts nor patch identity is ever used **alone**"), §1.7.3 carried-by-unit.

**Evidence:** "The squash text rule uses neither alone: it requires patch identity (to fix Tip − Tb = End − Bu per line) **and** counts over the unit's own diff."

**Analysis.**
- Tip and Tb are concrete commits (M and S^1), so Tip(L) − Tb(L) can be read directly from their blobs.
- Under UR-16, the identity Tip(L) − Tb(L) = End(L) − Bu(L) makes the carried check exactly equivalent to a pure count test on the squash commit's own diff:
  - S_A(L) ≤ max(0, Tip(L) − Tb(L));
  - min(S_R(L), Bu(L)) ≤ max(0, Tb(L) − Tip(L)).
- Patch identity adds no landing evidence beyond what it already contributes to every squash, namely selecting the integration (M3–M5). Its role in the rule is to let the check be phrased over Bu→End instead of Tb→Tip. Both phrasings are net line counts with no provenance (L-4).
- The landing conclusion for squash text paths with r ≠ e therefore rests on line counts. Calling this "not alone" is an interpretation of the owner's brief, not a demonstrated fact.

**Counterexample to the judgement.** Take FX-K23aj (C adds L; the target edited another line of f). Replace the carried check with "S_A ≤ Tip(L) − Tb(L)". Every input gives the same verdict under UR-16. No case distinguishes "patch identity plus counts" from "counts on S's diff".

**Impact.** Whether the revision cycle should stop is an owner-level reading of the brief. The packet settles it in the author's favour, and §0/§12 forbid that kind of self-authorisation. If the owner reads the brief strictly, every squash text path with r ≠ e must fall back to state equality, i.e. K33. That is the §2.4 fallback, and its cost (L-21) is unmeasured even though SPSM [M] is 3/3 squash.

**Smallest correction.**
- Add an explicit owner question (e.g. UR-21): "Accept count-level landing for squash text paths with r ≠ e (carried check), or adopt state-equality-only now?"
- Present the equivalence above verbatim.
- Mark the squash r ≠ e text branch O(UR-21) until the owner answers.

### R11-03 — MEDIUM — FX-K34 is unreachable as written; K34 cannot occur in first-integration squash under the P-02 PDB

**Section:** §1.7.3 Step 1; FX-K34; L-22; §3 coverage (K34 counted as binding).

**Proof.**
- In first-integration squash, Bu = B_X, the unique merge-base of an endpoint X and T, and Pre ⊑ C ⊑ X.
- Pre = C^1 is either in T, or in E (E = rev-list C … --not T).
- The endpoint check therefore guarantees merge-base --all Pre T = {m}. If Pre ⊑ T, then m = Pre.
- Every common ancestor of (Bu, Pre) is a common ancestor of (T, Pre), so it is ⊑ m.
- Also m ⊑ Pre ⊑ X and m ⊑ T, so m ⊑ Bu, which makes m a common ancestor of (Bu, Pre).
- Hence Fk = {m} = merge-base(Pre, T), which is always unique. K34 is reachable only:
  - for chained matches (Bu = X_v, with a criss-cross inside the branch);
  - for ancestry-merge; or
  - when P-02 is rejected.

**Counterexample.** In FX-K34's setup ("a criss-cross with Hb's history", |merge-base Hb Pre| = 2), Pre ∈ E has |merge-base Pre T| ≥ 2. The endpoint loop returns **K08** before FINALIZE.

**Impact.**
- FX-K34 (B) is misderived.
- K34 has no reachable binding fixture, so coverage is **59 / 50 / 9**, not 59 / 51 / 8.
- L-22's description is wrong for squash.
- On frequency (test 3): K34 is rare. It needs a branch-internal criss-cross or a P^1/Pre criss-cross.

**Smallest correction.**
- Rebuild FX-K34 as a chained match with a branch-internal criss-cross between X_v and Pre, or as an ancestry-merge with a criss-cross between P^1 and Pre. Alternatively, relabel it O(P-02).
- Restate L-22 accordingly.
- Note in §1.7.3 that for first-integration squash, Fk = merge-base(Pre, T).
